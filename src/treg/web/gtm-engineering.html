The seven jobs

What a GTM engineer actually runs

Each card is one job, the judgement call that makes it work, and the page with the prompt, the providers and a real run.

1. List building and people search

Search first, reveal second: some people searches are free and only the contact details cost. Cap every result set, because several providers bill per row.

2. Waterfall enrichment and work emails

Measure cost per correct email, not per row. Verify before you send and keep unknowns apart from invalids.

3. Buying signals and intent

A signal list is not a lead list. Most runs discard more than half before anyone looks up an email.

4. Account research and TAM

Resolve every company by its domain, not its name. A domain resolves cleanly; a name is ambiguous and can return the wrong company.

5. SEO and AI visibility

Price demand before you write: volume, CPC and the live results page, then decide which query is worth a page.

6. Competitor ads

Read the calls to action, not just the creative. In our run on one brand, 13 of its 20 live Meta ads asked for a sign-up.

7. Social listening and creators

Screen creators on engagement, not follower count. Our screening run computed it per account from recent posts before any outreach.

Questions

GTM engineering, answered plainly

What is a GTM engineer?

Someone who builds the systems that find, qualify and reach buyers: the lists, the enrichment, the signals that say who to contact now, and the automation that runs it every week. The job sits between RevOps, sales and engineering. In 2026 more of it runs from an agent such as Claude Code or Codex instead of a spreadsheet tool.

Do I need to write code?

No. You describe the job in a prompt, the agent chooses the calls, and it shows the price before it spends. Reading the result and deciding what to drop is the skill; the code is the agent's.

Should I use Clay or Claude Code?

Clay if your team wants a shared visual table and already pays for it. Claude Code with treg.to if you work in prompts and want to pay per call. On the 292-person test above, match quality was a tie (90.4% vs 89.7% exact) and the cost per correct email was $0.0056 against $0.0395.

Does treg.to choose the data provider for me?

The catalog lists every provider of a job side by side with measured success rates and prices, and your agent picks. Some jobs also have a routed endpoint (treg.<capability>) that chooses among providers when you call it; the result names the provider that answered.

What does a miss cost?

It depends on the provider. Some bill per successful result and some bill per call, found or not. Each catalog entry says which, and every run's receipt shows what was actually charged.

Can I use the provider keys my team already pays for?

Yes. Register the key once and calls to that provider go through it. A team's own key always wins over treg.to's, and those calls are never metered.
